Features of Database:

1. Time and Date:
Description: This feature represents the specific time and date when each data point was recorded.
Time Format: Each day is divided into 288 intervals, with each data entry recorded every 5 minutes.

Purpose: Helps track the periodicity and temporal patterns in the dataset, which can be crucial for analyzing daily glucose trends and other time-sensitive features.

2. Glucose:
Description: This represents the blood glucose levels recorded at each 5-minute interval.
Measurement Unit: Typically measured in mg/dL (milligrams per deciliter).

Purpose: Monitoring glucose levels is vital for managing diabetes, and fluctuations in this value can inform about hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia episodes.

3. Calories:
Description: This feature captures the number of calories consumed or burned during each interval.

Purpose: Caloric intake and expenditure are important for understanding energy balance, which can influence glucose metabolism and insulin requirements.

4. Heart Rate:
Description: The heart rate of the individual, measured in beats per minute (BPM), recorded every 5 minutes.

Purpose: Heart rate data is useful for correlating physiological stress or exercise with glucose levels and overall metabolic activity.

5. Steps:
Description: This represents the number of steps taken by the individual in each 5-minute interval.

Purpose: Physical activity has a direct impact on glucose regulation and insulin sensitivity, so tracking steps can help understand the effect of exercise on blood glucose levels.

6. Basal Rate:
Description: This refers to the continuous rate of insulin delivered by an insulin pump to maintain normal glucose levels when not eating.

Purpose: Basal insulin is crucial for managing background glucose levels and preventing hyperglycemia between meals or overnight.

7. Bolus Volume Delivered:
Description: This indicates the volume of insulin delivered during a meal or to correct high blood sugar levels, in response to carbohydrate intake or high glucose readings.

Purpose: Bolus insulin is essential for controlling glucose spikes post-meals or for correcting elevated glucose.

8. Carb Input:
Description: The amount of carbohydrates consumed at each time point, typically measured in grams.

Purpose: Carbohydrate intake is a key factor in determining bolus insulin requirements, as it directly affects postprandial glucose levels.
